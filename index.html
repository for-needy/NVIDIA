In one of the most dramatic reversals in modern corporate history, Nvidia’s Board of Directors unanimously voted on Monday, November 23, 2026, to reinstate co-founder Jensen Huang as president, chief executive officer, and board director, effective immediately.

The stunning comeback comes just eleven days after Huang abruptly stepped down on November 12. Within minutes of the announcement, Nvidia’s stock skyrocketed by more than 30%, adding roughly a trillion dollars in market value and pulling the broader U.S. stock market out of a week-long slump.

How Nvidia Reached the Brink—and Reversed Course

To understand the magnitude of Monday’s announcement, it helps to look back at how rapidly the crisis unfolded.

In late October 2026, reports surfaced regarding foreign intelligence targeting. By November 8, Nvidia’s board announced an independent investigation into reports that Huang had been targeted by an intelligence operation, though it confirmed that no national security or trade secrets had been compromised.

Shortly thereafter, internal pressure mounted, and on November 12, Nvidia announced Huang’s immediate resignation, appointing longtime CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO and naming Harvey C. Jones as Board Chair.

Without its visionary founder, however, Nvidia’s stock tumbled toward a 52-week low of $125.53 by Friday, November 20. Over the weekend, the board held a series of direct consultations with Huang and federal officials. Convinced of Huang’s transparency and reassured that his return posed zero security risk, the board voted unanimously to bring him back.

New Governance Guardrails

To protect shareholders and prevent future disruption, Nvidia announced three institutional checks:

  1. Independent Investigation: The board's review into intelligence targeting continues with Huang's full cooperation.
  2. National Security Committee: A dedicated committee of independent directors will directly oversee trade compliance and security risks.
  3. Chief Compliance Officer: Nvidia has commenced a search for a compliance head reporting directly to the board.

Similar to OpenAI's brief ouster of Sam Altman in 2023, Nvidia's board determined that navigating the artificial intelligence buildout without its founder posed far greater risk than reinstating him under tighter oversight.
